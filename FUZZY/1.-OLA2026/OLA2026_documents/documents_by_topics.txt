## Key papers grouped by topic

Below is a structured guide to the most relevant papers for your three questions, with a short note on what each contributes.

### A. Fuzzy adaptive PSO and metaheuristics (design, inertia weight, performance)

| Focus | Key contribution & relevance | Citations |
|-------|-----------------------------|-----------|
| Fuzzy adaptive PSO (classic) | Introduces a fuzzy system to dynamically adapt PSO inertia weight based on swarm performance; shows gains over fixed/linear inertia, especially on asymmetric/dynamic environments [1]| [1]|
| Per‑particle fuzzy self‑tuning PSO (FST‑PSO) | Multiple fuzzy rules map particle distance/fitness to per‑particle inertia, cognitive/social factors and velocity bounds; essentially settings‑free PSO, with faster convergence and competitive/better accuracy vs PSO, DE, CMA‑ES, ABC, GA [2]| [2]|
| MIMO fuzzy controller in PSO (MFCPSO) | Multiple‑input multiple‑output fuzzy logic controls acceleration coefficient and elite‑selection proportion, using both fitness and novelty; strong improvements on CEC 2017, especially large‑scale multimodal functions [3]| [3]|
| Fuzzy adaptive inertia in GA–PSO hybrid | Mamdani fuzzy system adapts inertia weight to control diversity in a GA–PSO hybrid, targeting fast yet non‑premature convergence [4]| [4]|
| Fuzzy signatures for inertia (Signature PSO) | Uses a fuzzy “signature” (structured linguistic representation of particle state) to adjust inertia weight in LQR tuning; shows better control performance vs other inertia strategies [5]| [5]|
| Fuzzy‑search Bat algorithm (ASF‑BA) | Uses Sugeno‑type fuzzy search and adaptive inertia in Bat Algorithm; outperforms BA, DE, PSO on CEC 2017 and NN training, showing fuzzy parameter control generalizes beyond PSO [6]| [6]|
| Comparative fuzzy inertia vs other schemes | Directly compares fuzzy adaptive inertia with constant, random, linear, nonlinear schedules; fuzzy scheme plus co‑evolution improves performance on 11 benchmark functions [7]| [7]|
| Interval type‑2 fuzzy PSO parameters | Compares different membership function types in interval type‑2 fuzzy system for PSO parameter adaptation; shows MF shape strongly affects optimization quality [8]| [8]|

### B. Metaheuristic tuning of fuzzy membership functions (linguistic granularity & semantics)

| Focus | Key contribution & relevance | Citations |
|-------|-----------------------------|-----------|
| PSO‑tuned fuzzy controllers (water tank, inverted pendulum) | Uses several PSO variants (with inertia, constriction) to optimize membership functions for fuzzy controllers; shows PSO‑based tuning greatly improves control accuracy and illustrates effect of MF parametrization [9]| [9]|
| Bee Colony tuning of MFs | BCO metaheuristic optimizes number/shape of membership functions in a real‑world energy‑consumption estimator; tuned MFs significantly increase precision vs expert‑designed sets [10]| [10]|
| PSO‑trained neuro‑fuzzy classifier with linguistic hedges | Membership functions built from piecewise sections modified by linguistic hedge operators; PSO learns hedge strengths and consequents, achieving better classification than competing fuzzy/neuro‑fuzzy methods, highlighting value of richer linguistic semantics and MF flexibility [11]| [11]|

### C. Linguistic information granulation and scale consistency (granularity & semantic representation)

| Focus | Key contribution & relevance | Citations |
|-------|-----------------------------|-----------|
| Linguistic information granulation + PSO (TSOG‑PSO) | Proposes linguistic information‑based granulation models for distributed linguistic preference relations, solved by a specialized PSO; shows how different granulation/control factors reshape the optimization landscape and decision outcomes [12]| [12]|
| Penalty‑based co‑evolutionary PSO for linguistic granulation | Defines a granulation model balancing consistency and consensus in distributed linguistic preferences, solved via co‑evolutionary PSO; demonstrates that linguistic granulation choices impact optimization and group decisions [13]| [13]|
| Multi‑granularity linguistic scale consistency | Analyzes how different linguistic granularities can produce inconsistent aggregation/ranking; proposes an attitude‑based representation to maintain consistency, directly evidencing that granularity and semantic scales affect decision and aggregation behavior [14]| [14]|

### D. Non‑fuzzy but relevant adaptive PSO baselines

These are useful comparison points for adaptive inertia without fuzzy logic:

- Stability‑based adaptive inertia weight (SAIW): dimension‑wise adaptive inertia and acceleration based on stability analysis and distance to pbest, significantly improving PSO on many benchmarks [15].  
- Bayesian adaptive inertia (BPSO): uses Bayesian techniques plus Cauchy mutation to adapt inertia, improving accuracy and convergence vs other inertia strategies [16].

They help isolate what fuzzy/linguistic design adds beyond numeric adaptation.

## How to use this set

- For **fuzzy inertia and parameter adaptation in PSO/metaheuristics**: start with [1], [2], [3], [7], [6], [4], [5], [8].  
- For **effects of membership function design and linguistic semantics**: emphasize [8], [11], [10], [9].  
- For **linguistic granularity and semantic representation in optimization/decision settings**: focus on [12], [13], [14], with [11], [8]as supporting technical evidence.

This set covers both algorithmic mechanisms (how fuzzy controllers are built) and semantic aspects (granularity, hedges, type‑2 sets) that influence adaptation behavior.
