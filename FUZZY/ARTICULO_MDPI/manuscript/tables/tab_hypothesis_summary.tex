\textbf{RQ1.} Does higher granularity improve configuration ranks? & Friedman $p=1.56\times10^{-200}$; Nemenyi CD $=6.59$. Best mean rank at $n=3/5/7/9$: 4.77/6.97/8.20/8.43. & Configuration ranks differ overall; the best mean rank worsens as $n$ increases. FCS-3-R1 and PSO share a Nemenyi group. & The omnibus test compares complete configurations and does not isolate an independent granularity effect. \\
\addlinespace[5pt]
\textbf{RQ2.} How do rankings vary across classes and dimensional regimes? & Class-level Kendall $\tau$: 0.867 (unimodal/scalable), 0.083 (unimodal/fixed), and 0.080 (scalable/fixed). & Concordance is high between the two scalable classes and weak between either and the fixed-dimensional group. & These comparisons describe the evaluated benchmark groups; they do not establish causal class effects or general transferability. \\
\addlinespace[5pt]
\textbf{RQ3.} Does granularity consistently reduce differences among rule patterns? & Function-level median SD across R1--R8 at $n=3/5/7/9$: ranks 8.884/8.366/8.715/9.374; log-gap 0.912/1.170/1.173/0.901. & Neither dispersion measure decreases consistently with granularity. & Descriptive evidence only; no factorial interaction test is reported. \\
\addlinespace[5pt]
\textbf{RQ4.} How does each fixed FCS configuration compare with PSO? & FCS-3-R1: 30/30/2 nominal wins/non-significant/losses; 16/46/0 after Bonferroni correction over 1,984 tests. & Across all configurations, 22 wins, 906 non-significant comparisons, and 1,056 losses remain after correction. & Non-significance does not establish equivalence. Choosing the best FCS separately for each instance is an oracle comparison. \\
\addlinespace[5pt]
\textbf{RQ5.} Do the best-ranked configurations transfer between $S_1$ and $S_2$? & $\tau(S_1,S_2)=0.095$, $p=0.451$; mean-gap leaders: $S_1$ FCS-3-R5, $S_2$ FCS-5-R6. & The subset leaders differ and observed rank concordance is weak. & The non-significant association is not evidence of independence and applies only to this evaluated split. \\
